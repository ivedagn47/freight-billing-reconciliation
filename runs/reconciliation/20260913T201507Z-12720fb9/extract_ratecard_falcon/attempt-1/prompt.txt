**Agreement ref:** FF/BFC/2024-11
**Between:** BlueFin Commerce ("the Customer") and Falcon Freight Pvt Ltd ("the Carrier")
**Service:** full and part truckload road carriage, all-India lanes

1. Base freight is charged **per kilometre of the booked lane distance**,

   - up to and including 500 kg: **₹18.00 per km**

2. **Express service**, where booked, carries a premium of **15% of the base
   freight**.
3. A **fuel surcharge of 12%** applies to the freight charge (base freight
   plus express premium, where applicable).

## Accessorial charges

4. **Residential delivery** (consignee address is a residence, or the
   booking is flagged residential): **₹250.00 flat per consignment**.
5. No other accessorial charges are payable under this agreement. Any
   service outside this schedule requires the Customer's written agreement
   in advance, evidenced by a rate confirmation referencing this agreement.

## Invoicing and payment

6. The Carrier invoices fortnightly. Payment is due 30 days from invoice
   date.
7. Billing disputes must be raised within 45 days of invoice date. Disputed
   line items are payable only on resolution; undisputed items remain due.
8. The Carrier may issue **credit notes** correcting earlier invoices. A
   credit note references the original consignment note number and states
   the invoice it corrects.

## Ground rules for this extraction

1. Every numbered clause in this contract (each top-level "N." list item) is a citable unit. 2. Represent weight/distance bands literally, including their boundary operators. If the contract says "under 50 kg" and "over 50 kg" (or similar strict-inequality language on both sides), encode that as two bands whose boundary operators are strict ("<" and ">") -- do NOT widen either operator to "<=" or ">=" to make the bands meet. If this leaves a value (e.g. exactly 50 kg) covered by neither band, that is a real gap in the contract, not a mistake in your extraction. Do not invent a rule to fill it. Instead, add an entry to "ambiguities" describing exactly what value(s) are uncovered and by which clause. Apply this same literal-boundary discipline to every band in the contract, not just an example that happens to look like this one -- check every band boundary against its neighbors for gaps as a discrete exercise, including boundaries that look closed on both sides (e.g. "up to and including X" next to "X+1 to Y") where a fractional value between them could still fall in neither band.

3. Do not resolve genuine ambiguity by picking the interpretation that seems more sensible. If the contract's wording leaves something underspecified -- how a condition is measured, what "calendar month" means for an invoice that doesn't align with one, whether two clauses interact -- put it in "ambiguities" with a clear description and the relevant clause, and represent the rest of the rate card without depending on your resolution of it.
